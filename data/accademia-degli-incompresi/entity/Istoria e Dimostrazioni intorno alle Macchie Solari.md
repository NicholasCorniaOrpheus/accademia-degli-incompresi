# Source Library overview

The text is structured as a series of letters and arguments that systematically dismantle the theory that sunspots are celestial bodies orbiting the Sun. Galileo emphasizes that physical reality must take precedence over established philosophical or theological traditions. He rejects the use of artificial, abstract models that ignore the data provided by the telescope, arguing that names and attributes must be derived from the essence of the objects themselves rather than imposed upon them.

Throughout the work, Galileo uses mathematical demonstrations to prove that the movement of sunspots is consistent with the rotation of the Sun on its own axis. He addresses the professional risks of his findings, noting the resistance he faces from those who prefer to err with the majority rather than accept new evidence. The final portion of the text explicitly rejects judicial astrology and calls for a physical understanding of the heavens based on observable phenomena.
