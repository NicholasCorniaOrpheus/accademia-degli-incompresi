## Overview from Source Library

The work is structured as a systematic critique and reconstruction of natural philosophy. Patrizi begins by establishing light as a substantial entity rather than a mere quality, positioning it as the fundamental instrument of divine creation and physical nourishment. He moves from this metaphysical foundation to a broader cosmological model that rejects the finite, geocentric structures of traditional scholasticism.

In the latter portions of the text, Patrizi addresses the physical composition of the universe. He argues for the existence of infinite space and provides alternative explanations for phenomena such as tides and the movement of the Earth. By treating the universe as a living, animated entity, he departs from the mechanical view of matter, advocating instead for a philosophy where life and light are the primary components of all existence.
