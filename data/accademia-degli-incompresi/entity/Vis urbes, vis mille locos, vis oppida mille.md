description: |-
  Introductory poem to Apian's geographical and astronomical treatise. Translation: You want cities, you want thousands of places, you want thousands of towns,
  Everything is not a great closed book to see? 
  You want to know in what place, or in what country he died
  Persian, Brabantine while you alone worship
  Persian, or Hyrcanus, or painted with the bow of nobility
  Parthius, and in the shelter of the Turks, proud of the horse?
  The force also of the tracts of the earth, and the paths of the heavens,
  And clear starry signs to mark the pole? 
  Do you want a big treasure bought for a small amount of money? Buy this and read it regularly as a Reader.

text: |-
  Vis urbes, vis mille locos, vis oppida mille,
  Omnia non magno clausa videre libro? 
  Vis quo scire loco, vel qua regione moretur
  Persa, Brabantinum dum colis ipse solum,
  Persa, vel Hyrcanus, vel picto nobilitis arcu
  Parthus, et in refugo Turca superbus equo?
  Vis etiam terrae tractus, coelíque meatus,
  Claraque stellifero signa notare polo? 
  Vis tibi thesaurum magnum paruo aere redemptum?
  Hoc eme, et assidue perlege Lector opus.
