# Source Library overview

The text is organized into two primary observational reports. The first part focuses on the Moon and the composition of the Milky Way and nebulae. Galileo argues that the Moon is not a perfect sphere but a body with physical features comparable to the Earth, such as craters and mountains. He uses these observations to reject the long-standing philosophical distinction between the corruptible Earth and the immutable heavens.

The second part details the discovery of four satellites orbiting Jupiter. Galileo provides a chronological log of his observations, noting the positions of these bodies over several weeks. He uses this data to demonstrate that the Earth is not the sole center of motion in the universe. By observing the relative speeds of these satellites, he also notes the correlation between their orbital distance from Jupiter and their period of revolution.
